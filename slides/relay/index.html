The bottleneck

One hub qubit, and everyone has to visit it

0 / 20 hub pulses
 

A two-qubit gate means bringing two atoms side by side in the entangling zone. The hub is one atom, so its partners queue: up, pulse, down, next. And every gate block needs that twice.

At 50 qubits: 49 partners × 2 passes = 98 pulses in a row on one wire, per layer. We had 294 written down as a hard floor for the depth of the 3-layer circuits.

The benchmark circuits are Max-Cut ansätze. They have one reference qubit that interacts with every other qubit: a hub. On this machine a two-qubit gate means physically carrying two atoms next to each other in the entangling zone. The hub is a single atom, so the partners queue: one comes up, pulse, goes back down, next. And each gate block needs that twice, with a rotation in between. At 50 qubits that is 98 pulses in a row on one wire, per layer. We wrote that down as a lower bound for depth, and every schedule we built was stuck on it.

The insight

The hub only needs the parity of its partners

Talking to all partners means: “flip the sign if I am 1 and an odd number of you are 1.” Parity is something the partners can work out among themselves.

hub pulses: 1  ·  partner pulses: 2 (off the hub’s wire)

ExactAn identity, not an approximation. P is a controlled-Y between two partners.

Chains upL hands to R, R hands on: any tree of partners funnels its parity into one root.
Here is the observation. What the hub does with all its partners is: flip a sign if the hub is one and an odd number of partners are one. It only cares about their parity, and parity is something the partners can work out among themselves. On the left the hub talks to L, then to R. [next] On the right, L first hands its bit to R with one gate between the two partners, the hub talks to R only, and then we undo the handover. [next] It is exactly the same operation, built only from the machine's own gates. The hub's wire went from two pulses to one.

The mechanism

All handovers at once. The hub meets only roots.

Pairs leave the hub ½ of its pulses, paths of four ¼. The rest runs in parallel, off its wire.

One pair alone saves nothing. The win is that all handovers happen at once. We line the partners up in a band in storage, lift the whole band in one move, and one pulse does every pair. A second level, shifted by one slot, merges pairs into paths of four. Now each group has one root that holds the parity, and the hub meets only the roots: here three visits instead of ten. Then the same lifts run in reverse to undo the relay. That is half or a quarter of the hub's pulses, and what remains is parallel work.

A gate block is one rotation between two pulses

Every two-qubit block of the input is a single rotation about Z⊗Y. In the right frame it is pulse, rotate, pulse.

The rotation happens in storageBetween the two pulses the target drops to storage for rx(θ). That visit is what the movement plan is built around.

Draft. Every block of the input circuit is one rotation about Z on the control and Y on the target. If every qubit is conjugated by S once, the block becomes pulse, rotate the target, pulse, with no basis change per block.

Draft · circuit identity 2 of 6

Blocks that share the hub share its pulses

Hub blocks that are ready together commute, so their pulses can move to the outside.

one block after the other: 3 separate storage visits
=
fan-out, one storage visit for all, fan-out again
∏p cz(hub, p) · rxp(θp) · cz(hub, p)  =  F · ∏p rxp(θp) · F   with  F = ∏p cz(hub, p)
Why it holdsEvery block is a Z⊗X rotation with Z on the hub, so they all commute.
What it buysThe hub stays in the zone for the whole batch, and all partners rotate together in storage.
It sets up the relayThe fan-out F is exactly the piece the relay identity replaces.
Draft. All hub blocks that are ready at the same time commute. So instead of pulse, rotate, pulse for each partner in turn, we do one fan-out over all partners, rotate all of them in one storage visit, and fan out again.

A whole tree of partners funnels into one root

Chain the handovers: after two levels, one atom holds the parity of four.

hub pulses: 4
=
hub pulses: 1  ·  partner pulse layers: 4 (2 levels in, 2 out; each is one lift)
∏p cz(hub, p)  =  P† · cz(hub, R) · P   P = the Y gates, children first; each is rx(−π/2)·cz·rx(π/2)
Why it holdsA controlled-Y turns the parent’s Z into child·parent. After the tree, the root’s Z is the parity of all four.
Levels are parallelThe gates of one level act on disjoint pairs: one band lift and one pulse per level.
Two levels fit the machineA path of four sits in one storage row. Level 2 is the same lift shifted by one slot.
Draft. The handover chains. In a path of four, the two outer atoms hand to the inner ones, then one inner atom hands to the other. That last atom, the root, now carries the parity of all four, and the hub needs one pulse instead of four.

Quarter-turn blocks need one pulse, not two

When the angle is ±π/2 the block is a Clifford gate, and one pulse with local gates is enough.

=
1 pulse. The control’s S is diagonal and simply waits

Where it appliesIn the warm-start circuits every non-zero angle is ±π/2, so every block there takes this form.

Draft. When a block's angle is plus or minus a quarter turn, the block is a Clifford gate and one pulse with local gates is enough. In the warm-start circuits every non-zero angle is like that.

The rotation between the two pulses can sit on the control instead of the target. Only local gates change.

Draft. The rotation between the two pulses can sit on either atom; only the local gates around the block change. So when the hub is the target, its partner does the rotating and the hub never has to leave the zone.

Draft · circuit identity 6 of 6

Three small identities that save trips

None of them is deep. Each one removes moves or gates in every round.

A pulse twice is no pulse
=
Two bystanders on one pair can ride along with a band and take both pulses. The band stays one rigid block and nobody idles in the zone.
Diagonal gates can wait
=
An rz commutes with the pulse, so it never forces a trip to storage on its own. It waits until the atom is there anyway.
Two spellings of one gate
=
The compiler emits whichever is shorter, for example rz(π)·rx(θ)·rz(−π) = rx(−θ): one gate instead of three.

cz · cz = 1  ·  rz · cz = cz · rz  ·  rz(φ) rx(θ) rz(λ) = rz(φ−π) rx(−θ) rz(λ+π)

Draft. Three small identities. A pulse applied twice cancels, so two bystander atoms can ride along with a band. Diagonal gates commute with the pulse, so an rz never forces a storage trip. And every single-qubit gate has two Euler spellings; the compiler emits the shorter one.
